Documented, on `~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.spectra_from_phase_spaces`,
that a single grid cell is usually the wrong probe volume. A Thomson scattering
volume at 532 nm is tens to a hundred microns; a PIC cell in a shock run is a
few. Averaging the phase space over the cells the real collection volume spans
costs nothing, is what the measurement being modelled actually does, and buys
:math:`\sqrt{N}` in macroparticle statistics exactly where the forward model is
most sensitive to them -- the tail, which is where the electron feature reads.

On ``R1_paper_470eV`` the probe cell holds 12,000-47,000 macroparticles until
the shock passes and 2,000-5,000 afterwards, and the noise in the published
spectrogram begins at the frame where that drop happens. Averaging over 11
cells -- 84 microns, a realistic probe volume -- takes the residual roughness
in the EPW wing from 0.25 to 0.06 dex and raises the frames with a resolved
electron feature from 24/51 to 36/51.
