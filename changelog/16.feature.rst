`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.spectra_from_phase_spaces` takes
``epw_notches="auto"``, which sizes the stray-light mask from the central
feature as measured in the IAW window, at every timestep, instead of holding it
at a fixed pair of wavelengths. A fixed mask does not stay over a feature the
flow is Doppler-shifting: at :math:`2\times10^{6}` m s\ :sup:`-1` the shift is
:math:`\lambda^2 k u / 2 \pi c \approx 3.7` nm, most of a 4 nm notch, and the
bin immediately outside then holds the skirt of a feature orders of magnitude
above the electron-plasma-wave satellites -- which on one OSIRIS run took it
from 0.5% of the window's area before the piston arrived to 88--98% after. The
interval applied at each timestep is recorded in ``meta["epw_notch_applied"]``.

The mask is also capped at ``notch_max_fraction`` of the Bohm--Gross satellite
offset, since a mask must not eat what it is protecting: where the plasma is
only weakly collective the central feature and the electron feature merge, and
sizing purely by containment masked 30 nm of the window -- the satellites with
it.

`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.spectra_from_phase_spaces` results span many
decades, so ``tools/pic_thomson_osiris_comparison.py`` now draws its
spectrograms on a log colour scale by default (``--no-log-scale`` for the old
one). On a linear scale the skirt of the central feature is the only thing
visible and everything else reads as black.
