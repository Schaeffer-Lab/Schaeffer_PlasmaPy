`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.condition_phase_space` now takes its boxcar
width and its taper rolloff in **thermal speeds** rather than in velocity bins,
as ``smoothing_width`` and ``max_taper_width``, and sizes them against the
narrowest appreciably populated slice it is given. Smoothing is a convolution,
so a boxcar of :math:`W` bins applied :math:`n` times adds
:math:`n (W^2 - 1) \Delta v^2 / 12` to the second moment of *every* slice
whatever its own width -- an additive temperature, and the forward model reads
the thermal speed straight off the distribution. How much that costs depends on
how wide the code's velocity grid happens to be, which is a choice in the input
deck rather than anything about the plasma: on an OSIRIS run whose momentum
diagnostic spanned :math:`\pm 0.71 c` while its electrons occupied
:math:`\pm 0.03 c`, ``smoothing_window=40`` over three passes turned a 38.8 eV
ambient into 813 eV and moved :math:`\alpha` from 12.0 to 3.2 -- and cost only
18% later in the same run, so the bias does not cancel between two measurements.
`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.smooth_vdf` gained a ``variance_warning``,
which `~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.condition_phase_space` enables by
default, so a window given in bins now says what it cost. The bin-valued
``smoothing_window`` and ``max_taper_bins`` still work and are unchanged.
