`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.spectra_from_phase_spaces` now says whether
the electron feature it returns is a measurement at all. The EPW satellite sits
at the Bohm--Gross resonance, so it reads :math:`f_e` at
:math:`\sqrt{\alpha^2 + 3}` thermal speeds; a PIC histogram is populated only as
far as its last macroparticle, and reaching :math:`n \sigma` needs of order
:math:`e^{n^2/2}` particles per cell, so three to five is typical and no amount
of compute reaches twelve. Beyond it the taper and the floor supply the tail and
the resonance of :math:`|1 - \chi_e/\epsilon|^2` amplifies that into a satellite
which looks like data, tracks nothing, and moves when ``max_taper_bins`` moves.
The two numbers are recorded per timestep as ``epw_tail_ratio`` and
``epw_tail_required`` on
`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.ThomsonSpectrogram`, reduced to
``epw_resolved``, and written to and read back from HDF5. Since
`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.extend_vdf_tail` fits the tail rather than
tapering it, a resonance outside the sampled data is an extrapolation rather
than an artefact, and is kept and priced instead of hidden:
``epw_tail_uncertainty`` is the factor by which the amplitude there is
uncertain, from the standard error of the fitted tail width. On a real OSIRIS
shock the resonance runs out to 17.4 thermal speeds against data reaching 3.4,
and the fit still holds the amplitude to a factor of 3.2. Pass
``mask_unresolved_epw=True`` to blank those rows instead, which is what you want
with ``tail_model=None``, where the tail really is a property of the taper.

Reconstructed populations are handled separately: they have no last
macroparticle, so the limit is wherever ``velocity_headroom`` stopped, and the
default of 6 covers the resonance only to :math:`\alpha \approx 5.7`.
`~plasmapy.diagnostics.pic_thomson.read_warpx_hybrid_electrons` therefore takes
``velocity_headroom`` too, and ``meta["epw_tail_analytic"]`` names the
populations whose tail is assumed rather than sampled.
