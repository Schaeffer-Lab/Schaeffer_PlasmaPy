`~plasmapy.diagnostics.thomson.arbitrary_chi` now evaluates the principal-value
integral for :math:`\chi` in closed form, and is exact for the piecewise-linear
interpolant of :math:`f'` that it was always integrating. The quadrature it
replaces had three faults.

- It was first order in ``nPoints`` on a grid anchored at :math:`\xi`, so on a
  noisy PIC distribution it rang at the sample spacing as :math:`\xi` swept the
  wavelength axis: 0.56 dex in the EPW wings of a KinShock run and 0.64 dex in
  the IAW wings at the default ``nPoints=1e3``, and 0.26 dex across the
  satellites of an OSIRIS run.
- Its window was :math:`\xi` plus or minus the span of the velocity axis, which
  misses part or all of an ion distribution whenever :math:`|\xi|` exceeds the
  span -- which in an EPW window it always does. No ``nPoints`` reduced this; at
  ``1e5`` it still left 0.17 dex in late KinShock frames.
- An odd ``nPoints`` gave the two sides of the singularity different spacings,
  so the standoff terms stopped cancelling: 22.7% error in :math:`\chi` at 1001,
  against 4.9% at 1000.

The closed form agrees with a brute-force quadrature at :math:`h/2000` to
:math:`4\times10^{-11}` on a Maxwellian and :math:`4\times10^{-8}` on a noisy
histogram, recovers the large-:math:`\xi` asymptote, and makes the forward model
about 40 times faster end to end on a 51-frame run -- 27 s to under a second --
with no large temporaries. ``phi``, ``nPoints``, ``inner_range`` and
``inner_frac`` are still accepted and are now unused. Both numba kernels are
compiled with ``cache=True``, which removes an 8.5 s compile from every process.
